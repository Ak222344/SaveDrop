Build a Prelocation Pit

Join this 7-day challenge to combat water scarcity! You’ll learn how to build a percolation pit (a simple underground structure) or recharge well (a deeper groundwater refill system) step by step. By redirecting rainwater into the ground, you’ll help replenish Earth’s water table—while earning points for every action!

    Install a Rain Barrel System

    Rain barrels collect roof runoff for garden use, reducing demand on potable water. Outdoor water use accounts for up to 50% of total household water consumption, especially during warmer months. A single rain barrel can collect up to 1,300 gallons of water per year, which helps reduce: Your dependence on potable (drinking) water for irrigation Stormwater runoff that can carry pollutants into local waterways Your water bill and environmental footprint By completing this challenge, you’re actively reducing water waste, lowering demand on municipal systems, and building resilience during drought periods.

    What You'll do:

    • Measure your downspout area for barrel placement
      Buy a 50+ gallon rain barrel with mosquito-proof screen and spigot.
      Place barrel on a raised stand for gravity flow.
      Cut the downspout and attach diverter kit.
      Use water for outdoor non-edible plants or cleaning tools.

    • Perfect for:

    Homeowners with gardens or people living in area with seasonal rain!

    Choose your Rain Barrel System

    Buy a 50+ gallon rain barrel with a spigot, overflow outlet, and mosquito-proof screen.

    Pick an ideal installation spot

    Select a downspout near your gardern or where you can get water easily and the area should also be flat

    Create a stand for your barrel

    Elevate the barrel by 12-18 inches using a stand made up of bricks etc.

    Cut and Install the downspout diverter

    Use a hacksaw to cut your downspout about 1–2 feet above the top of the barrel. Attach the diverter kit that reroutes rainwater into the barrel and reconnects to the overflow drain.

    Secure the barrel and spigot

    Make sure the spigot is tightly sealed and test for leaks. Position the barrel to allow easy access to the spigot or hose connection. Ensure overflow is directed away from your house’s foundation.

    Use collected rainwater for use

    Connect a watering can or hose to the barrel. Water non-edible plants, trees, flower beds, or wash garden tools. Don’t use it for drinking or edible crops unless filtered and sanitized.
